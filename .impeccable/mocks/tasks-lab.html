Tasks — the reading that is also the filter

Pass 0 settled the page header and left one seam open, on this page. PageHeader’s measure slot carries a page’s own reading — 14 open  3 paused — and on eleven surfaces that reading is informational. On /tasks the same three words already exist and they are the status filter: TaskStatusStrip renders open · overdue · today with counts, and pressing a count narrows the list. The header is delegated to the client component for exactly that reason.

So: same idiom, same slot, two different contracts. Three ways out are drawn below over the whole page — capture line, filter bar, groups, rows — at both widths and in both themes. The page body is identical in all three. Only the header and the filter bar move.

One correction to the brief that opened this: the measure is sans 14, not mono. The strip is mono 12. They were never the same typeface — what they share is the slot and the shape of the phrase, which is enough for the collision to be real.

T1The measure, made interactive

The strip takes the measure slot and the header accepts that on this one page the reading is a control. Counts drop the mono and adopt the measure’s own sans-14 — figure at 500, word at 400 — so nothing teaches the reader that “mono means clickable”. Pressing one narrows the list; the active count takes ink and an accent underline. The scope narrows keep a bar of their own below.

What it costs. One filter set, split across the header boundary: status rides the title’s baseline as a header slot, project and domain sit on a bar of their own below it — two owners for two halves of one control that narrow the same list. At 393pt this quietly collapses: the header wraps its right cluster onto a second row, so the order becomes title → status → scope, which is exactly T2’s layout with 26px between the two halves instead of 12 — T1 degrades into T2 on a phone, minus the shared bar. And on both widths the underline spends the accent on “you are here” while the overdue count spends it on “this is late”: two things orange for two different reasons, on the same three words.

T2The filter bar, as it ships

The measure slot goes unused and the header is the page’s name alone. Everything that filters sits on one bar below it: the status strip exactly as it ships — mono 12, an accent underline on the active count, the alarm colour on a non-zero overdue — with the two scope narrows at the far end. The smallest change of the three.

What it costs. The strip still looks like a readout. It is bare text on the page’s quietest register, and nothing about it says press me until the cursor is over it — which is the same complaint that put this on a gate, just moved 40px down. The accent still does double duty: the active underline and the overdue alarm are the same orange on the same line. And a 36px headline with nothing on its baseline is a wide empty line on the app’s second-busiest page.

measure slot empty · strip mono 12, unchanged · active ink + 2px accent underline · overdue accent when non-zero · one bar, status left, scope right

T3The segmented controlrecommended

The strip stops borrowing the measure’s clothes and becomes what it is. DESIGN.md already names the voice filters speak in — “Control — mono, uppercase, 12px, 12px radius, 36px tall… row actions, form buttons, filters” — and the app already builds one segmented control in it: PriorityPicker, four exclusive cells in the task dialog. Status is the same interaction, so it takes the same geometry: a surface-2 trough, three cells, the active one lifted onto paper. The measure slot stays unused, and that becomes the rule — where a page’s reading is also its control, the reading goes with the control.

What it buys, and what it costs. Buys: the accent is freed to mean one thing again. The active cell is marked by the lift, so orange on this bar only ever says there is something late — which is the One Orange Rule kept rather than bent. Costs: it is the loudest of the three, a filled object where the page had only text, and it borrows the shell tab group’s mechanic one level down — three views of the docket sitting under five views of the app.

What to look for

1 · Switch to 393pt on T1 — it becomes T2 with a wider gap. Whatever T1 wins, it wins on desktop only.
2 · The orange, on each option’s filter row — count how many different things it is saying. T1 and T2 say two; T3 says one.
3 · T2’s header on desktop — 36px of name with an empty baseline, and whether that reads as calm or as unfinished.
4 · The rows, in all three — the ring replaces the P1–P4 badge, the domain dot moves into the left column beside the mark, and the title drops to 400 with P1 the one step up. Identical everywhere; not what is being voted on, but say so now if it is wrong.
5 · Dark, on T3 — the trough is --ground-2 (#2b2725) and the active cell is --paper (#232020), so on dark the selected cell is darker than its trough rather than lighter. That inversion is the tokens working as designed and the shell’s tab group already lives with it, but here it means the shadow is doing most of the marking. Judge whether it still reads.
