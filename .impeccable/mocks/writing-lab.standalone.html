Writing — measure, scale, leftover width

Pass 3 Phase 0. Passes 0–2 refined one object: a page of rows. Nothing they settled says anything about a paragraph. At 1092px the note body currently runs the full frame — roughly 150 characters per line, about twice a comfortable measure. The editor also invents three sizes the closed ramp never named (24 / 20 / 18 for title and headings).

The two axes are one gate: a larger type scale demands a wider measure, and a narrow measure makes a large scale look cramped. Each option below is a package — measure + type + what the leftover frame does — not a menu of independent knobs. Comp /notes/[id] and /chat under each. Desktop and phone, light and dark.

Then stop and pick. Do not build past this line until the package is closed.

The gate — three packages

Every package answers three things together: how wide a line of prose may be, which ramp steps the editor spends, and what the 72rem frame does with the leftover width. Chat inherits the same measure and the same body size; its speaker distinction is fixed across all three (see held decisions).

Held constant — not on the ballot

Header. The breadcrumb ← Notes is the editor’s header. The note’s name is editable content and cannot live in a static PageHeader. Today already sets the precedent that a surface may decline the page header for a reason; this is that reason, written down. /chat keeps its existing PageHeader (“Ask”) — its title is not content.

Chat registers. A person wrote the user message → sans body at 400, right-aligned, ink-2. The assistant’s answer is running prose → sans body at 400, left-aligned, full ink, on the measure. The current inversion (user = mono labelled, assistant = type-title 500) is rejected in every package. Distinction is alignment + ink step, not register swap.

Panels as rows. Backlink and Linked names rest at 400 (ADR-0044). Group labels are SectionHead 16/500, not mono eyebrows. Resolve / Delete / Send are control-voice buttons.

No new ramp sizes. The undocumented 24 / 20 leave in every package. W3 uses existing ramp steps more generously (Headline + Lead); it does not invent a fourth size.

W1Column left · body 16 · ground empty

Prose is a 65ch column, left-aligned on the frame’s edge. The leftover width is empty ground — breathing room, not a second region. Type stays on the closed ramp at reading size: title at Title (30/500), body at Body (16/400), in-body headings at Lead and Section (18/500 and 16/500). Backlinks and Linked sit below the note at full frame width, as list rows.

Cost: on a wide desk the right half of the frame is blank while you write. Panels only appear after a long scroll past the body — the same structural complaint as today, just with a readable measure. The blank is honest; it is also unused.

measure 65ch left · title 30 / 500 · body 16 / 400 / 1.6 · h2 18 / 500 · h3 16 / 500 · panels below, full frame · leftover empty ground

W2Column + rail · body 16 · leftover earns its keep recommend

Same measure and same type as W1. The leftover width stops being empty: Backlinks and Linked become a right rail on desktop — secondary navigation that sits beside the note instead of under it. On phone the rail stacks below, same component tree. Chat has no panels, so it is identical to W1 there; the package difference is entirely /notes/[id].

Cost: the first viewport of a note is busier. A short note with many links will look panel-heavy; a long note with none will look like W1 with a blank rail (the rail collapses when empty — drawn here populated). Sticky rail on a long body is a real benefit and a real temptation to fill it.

measure 65ch left · type identical to W1 · panels right rail 260px, stack on phone · leftover is the rail

W3Reading lead · body 18 · column centred

A note is a document, not a form field. Body steps to Lead (18/400, 1.7) — already on the ramp, currently spent only on pull-quotes. Title steps to Headline (36/500). In-body h2 takes Title (30/500); h3 takes Lead weight at 18. Measure widens to 72ch so the larger body does not feel pinched, and the column is centred in the frame so the leftover ground balances on both sides. Panels stay below, on the same centred measure.

Cost: body 18 is a different app register — denser lists at 16 and airy prose at 18 will be felt as two products if the jump is wrong. Centring a left-to-right writing surface fights the shell’s left edge and the breadcrumb. Headline 36 next to a breadcrumb can re-introduce the “page title” silhouette the Pass 0 header deleted.

measure 72ch centred · title 36 / 500 · body 18 / 400 / 1.7 · h2 30 / 500 · h3 18 / 500 · panels below, same measure · leftover balanced ground

What to look for

1 · Characters per line on the note body — turn the measure guide on. W1/W2 should land near 60–70 characters; if a line still feels like a ruler, the measure is wrong regardless of package.

2 · W1’s blank right — is empty ground a feature (focus) or a defect (unused frame)? That single feeling picks W1 vs W2.

3 · W2 rail on a long body — scroll the desk note. Do the panels stay useful beside the prose, or do they nag?

4 · W3 at body 18 next to chat — the assistant’s long answer is the same size as the note body. If 18 feels right for a note and heavy for chat (or the reverse), the package is fighting itself.

5 · Phone — measure collapses to the content width on every package. The only real phone difference is W2’s stack order (prose then panels). If phone is the decider, the gate was the wrong gate.

6 · Title vs breadcrumb — W3’s 36px title is the one most likely to feel like a page header. If it does, that is evidence against W3, not a reason to add a real PageHeader.
