The page header, three ways

Twelve surfaces repeat one header. Today cannot answer what replaces it — Today has no page header at all: its h1 is a sentence about the day and its dateline is the day nav. So the question is open, and it is settled once here rather than twelve times downstream.

One measured fact reframes it. The desktop tab group has five tabs — Today, Tasks, Notes, Links, More — and eight of the twelve pages sit behind More. On /projects the active pill says “More”, which names nothing. So the tab group is a coarse locator and the page header is the fine one; the header is not redundant on desktop, and the desktop/phone asymmetry the brief anticipated does not exist. What is genuinely open is only how much room the page name gets, and that is the single axis these three options move along — loud, balanced, silent. Every option carries the same four slots: title · measure · subtitle · action.

Two decisions are the same in all three, so they are not being voted on here — say so if either is wrong. The editorial second title is gone. “What’s in motion”, “Loose thoughts”, “The inbox” are linen-era copy: a poetic restatement for an operator who knows every route. The page is called Projects. The eyebrow never sits above the heading again — it stays alive everywhere it is a system label (the dateline, the tape heading, group labels), but stacking it over an h1 is the composition this pass is deleting.

3 options · /projects · /notes · /inbox

AHeadline

The page announces itself. h1 at 36px — one ramp step below Today’s hero, so a list page can never out-shout the day — with the measure as a quiet line beneath and the action riding the headline’s baseline. No rule: the 64px the shell already puts above is the separation.

h1 36 / 500 / −0.9px, phone 30 / 500 / −0.75px · measure under the title · action baseline-right · no divider · 30px to first content

BMeasured

The page opens with a reading, not just a name. h1 drops to 30px so the measure can sit on its baseline in Today’s own counters idiom — the numeral in ink at 500, the word at 14, anything late in the one orange. A single hairline closes the block, which is the only structure the legacy header had that was worth keeping.

h1 30 / 500 / −0.75px, unchanged on phone · measure + action on the baseline, wrapping to their own row at 393pt · 1px --line divider · 24px to first content

CLabel bar

No display type at all. The route name is something the system labelled, not something a person wrote, so it takes the mono eyebrow and the whole header collapses to one 12px bar over a hairline — name, measure, action. The page starts at its content. The densest option, and the one that trusts the operator most.

h1 mono 12 / 0.1em / uppercase / ink · measure joins the bar in mono 12 · action inline · 1px --line divider · 22px to first content

What to look for

Switch the width and the theme with the controls above; the three pages are the same in every panel.

1 · /projects at phone — nothing above the page names it but the header, and the dock says “More”.
2 · C against the group labels — “PROJECTS” and “ACTIVE” are the same 12px mono seven pixels apart. C’s title is ink and the labels are ink-4, which is the only thing separating them.
3 · /inbox — no measure to show, so each option has to degrade to title + subtitle without looking unfinished.
4 · /notes — the trailing action, and whether it reads as belonging to the page or to the first row beneath it.
5 · A on desktop — 36px twelve times, and whether it earns the room on a page whose content is 16px rows.
