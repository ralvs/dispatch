Dispatch’s own shape

The jerad-ops 2.0 read (docs/review-jerad-ops-v2-2026-08-15.html) asked what Dispatch could take from the reference. This document answers it, and the answer is mostly less. Jerad runs an agency and needs a defensible record of billable work. Renan is an employee whose project and ticket history already lives in Linear, inside the company’s walls. Half of what the reference models is a job Dispatch does not have.

The governing rule for this plan. Dispatch does not duplicate Linear. It holds the thinking around work, the life around work, and the things that quietly rot when nobody looks. Anything that exists to prove what was delivered to a paying client comes out.

01 Two apps that look alike and are not

The reference implementation and Dispatch share a vocabulary — domain, project, task, note. They do not share a job. Naming the difference is what makes the rest of this plan obvious rather than arbitrary.

What jerad-ops has to do

  • Prove what was delivered to a client, and when.
  • Hold hours against a quote, per retainer cycle.
  • Keep a per-project conversation and update log.
  • Track owned gear for insurance and resale.
  • Notice a client who has gone silent.

Every one of these is a record kept for someone else’s benefit.

What Dispatch has to do

  • Catch a thought before it is lost.
  • Hold the work thinking that is not yet a Linear ticket.
  • Hold home and family errands, meetings, and things to buy.
  • Notice a part of life nobody has touched in a while.
  • Keep the journal, quotes, notes and people around all of it.

Linear owns delivery history. Dispatch owns attention.

Consequence. The agency column is not a Dispatch feature that was never finished — it is a Dispatch feature that should never exist. activity_log, inventory_items, client_id, quoted_hours, hours_logged and engagement_type are all in that column.

02 The model, after this plan

Domains are the base point. A project is a loose bucket that tags tasks — nothing more. Everything else that behaved like a task gets retired, because three task shapes is two too many.

Why a project stops carrying progress of its own. A percentage that only moves when you tick an invented checklist item measures the checklist, not the work. Counting the project’s own tasks measures something that exists for other reasons and cannot be gamed by forgetting to tick.

03 A want is a task with the clock switched off

“Buy milk tomorrow” is an action with a time. “Buy a new wardrobe” is an intent with no time. Today they share one list, so the second one sits there looking overdue-ish forever and makes the list feel dishonest.

What it costs. One column, one predicate beside isOverdue in lib/task-predicates.ts, one filter chip on /tasks, and one exclusion in the Today read. It reuses the domain, the project tag, the notes field and the whole task form.

04 The neglect sweep, and where its output lands

This is the one repair the last report ranked first, and it stays first. Dispatch already promises a cron that watches for neglect. There is no such cron. The threshold editor on a domain writes a number that nothing reads, into a table with no rows and no writers.

05 What the pages are missing

Every list page today opens with a title and a count of rows. A count of rows is the one fact you can already get by looking at the rows. The pages that feel thin are thin because the header states the obvious and withholds the interesting.

The design collision, stated honestly

ADR-0042 settled what the page header may carry: a 36px title, and a measure — a figure and the word it counts, at a flat 14px. ADR-0046 added facts — identity attributes, also flat. Both decisions were deliberately quiet, so that “a list page can never out-shout the day”. A row of big numbers is a third reading type, and it does not fit either slot.

06 Routines: edit, and fix yesterday

Two gaps, one small and one that breaks a stated rule.

Editing a routine

There is no edit. The page offers create, archive and delete only, and deleting warns that the whole completion history goes with it — so a typo in a routine name currently costs you the streak. The server side is already there: UpdateRoutineSchema exists and is complete. What is missing is an updateRoutineAction and an edit mode on the row, exactly like the one /domains already has.

Ticking a past day

The service can already do it — setCompletion(sb, id, date, done) takes a date, and ToggleCompletionSchema accepts one. The server action deliberately refuses to: it derives the date from the server clock and its comment says the date is “never trusted from the client.” Backfill means letting the client name a date, which is a real reversal of that rule and needs to be written down, not slipped in.

How to keep the rule mostly intact. Accept a date from the client, then bound it server-side: it must be a real calendar date, not in the future, and not older than the 30 days the grid already shows. The client picks which of the visible squares to tick; it never gets to invent a date the UI is not already displaying. Streaks and rates recompute from the completion log, so no stored total goes stale.
app/(authed)/routines/actions.tsAdd updateRoutineAction; widen toggleCompletionAction to take a bounded date.
app/(authed)/routines/routine-row.tsxEdit mode on the row; the 30 grid squares become buttons instead of decoration.
lib/routine-stats.tsAlready computes streak, longest streak, 7-day count and the grid. Nothing to add for the band except the two percentages.

07 Retire now, drop later

Two of the answers pull against each other: milestones and the billing fields are agreed removals, but nothing is to be dropped from the database yet. Both are satisfiable — they are different phases, not the same one.

Phase A — retire in code (this plan)

  • Stop reading and writing the column or table.
  • Remove its UI, its form field, its service function.
  • Remove it from the row schema, so it leaves the select.
  • The rows stay in Postgres, untouched and reachable.

Reversible in an afternoon. Nothing is lost.

Phase B — drop in SQL (a later, separate patch)

  • One migration dropping whatever Phase A proved unnecessary.
  • Only after living without it long enough to be sure.
  • Its own ADR, following the ADR-0007 precedent.

Not scheduled. Not in this plan.

09 Still open

Six questions this plan could not settle on its own. Each carries a recommendation, so silence on any of them means the recommendation stands.

O1Is “don’t drop anything yet” about the database only?

Milestones and the billing fields were agreed removals, and then nothing was to be dropped. §07 reads that as: pull them out of the code now, leave the rows in Postgres, drop them in a later patch.

Recommended: yes — Phase A now, Phase B never scheduled until you have lived without it.

O2Which pages actually get a stat band?

Four are clear wins and three are guesses (§05). Adding a band to a page whose numbers you would never read is exactly the mistake the jerad-ops 2.0 release was written about.

Recommended: ship /routines, /domains and /tasks. Leave /projects, /journal and /notes until those three have been used for a while.

O3Does writing a note count as touching its domain?

Once notes carry a domain, they become a fifth candidate source for the neglect sweep’s last-touch date. Counting them makes a domain look tended when all you did was think about it. Not counting them means a week of notes about Health still reads as neglect.

Recommended: count it. Attention is what this measure is for, and thinking about something is attention.

O4How far back may a routine day be ticked?

The row already draws 30 days. A wider window needs a date picker and a reason.

Recommended: 30 days — exactly the squares already on screen, enforced server-side.

O5How many tasks does a project row show?

Open tasks only, or everything including done? Always visible, or behind a disclosure?

Recommended: open tasks only, up to five, then “+N more”. Done work belongs on the project page, not in the list.

O6Which ADRs does this plan owe?

The next free number is 0053 — 0052 is note attachments. Three decisions here are hard to reverse, surprising without context, and the result of a real trade-off, which is the bar the repo uses.

Recommended: one for the stat band as a third reading type against ADR-0042/0046; one for a client-named completion date on routines; one recording the jerad-ops 2.0 read — what was taken, what was refused, and that Linear owns delivery history. AGENTS.md requires the third one anyway.
