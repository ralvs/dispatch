Dispatch’s own shape

The jerad-ops 2.0 read (docs/review-jerad-ops-v2-2026-08-15.html) asked what Dispatch could take from the reference. This document answers it, and the answer is mostly less. Jerad runs an agency and needs a defensible record of billable work. Renan is an employee whose project and ticket history already lives in Linear, inside the company’s walls. Half of what the reference models is a job Dispatch does not have.

The governing rule for this plan. Dispatch does not duplicate Linear. It holds the thinking around work, the life around work, and the things that quietly rot when nobody looks. Anything that exists to prove what was delivered to a paying client comes out.

01 Two apps that look alike and are not

The reference implementation and Dispatch share a vocabulary — domain, project, task, note. They do not share a job. Naming the difference is what makes the rest of this plan obvious rather than arbitrary.

What jerad-ops has to do

  • Prove what was delivered to a client, and when.
  • Hold hours against a quote, per retainer cycle.
  • Keep a per-project conversation and update log.
  • Track owned gear for insurance and resale.
  • Notice a client who has gone silent.

Every one of these is a record kept for someone else’s benefit.

What Dispatch has to do

  • Catch a thought before it is lost.
  • Hold the work thinking that is not yet a Linear ticket.
  • Hold home and family errands, meetings, and things to buy.
  • Notice a part of life nobody has touched in a while.
  • Keep the journal, quotes, notes and people around all of it.

Linear owns delivery history. Dispatch owns attention.

Consequence. The agency column is not a Dispatch feature that was never finished — it is a Dispatch feature that should never exist. activity_log, inventory_items, client_id, quoted_hours, hours_logged and engagement_type are all in that column.

02 The model, after this plan

Domains are the base point. A project is a loose bucket that tags tasks — nothing more. Everything else that behaved like a task gets retired, because three task shapes is two too many.

Why a project stops carrying progress of its own. A percentage that only moves when you tick an invented checklist item measures the checklist, not the work. Counting the project’s own tasks measures something that exists for other reasons and cannot be gamed by forgetting to tick.

03 A want is a task with the clock switched off

“Buy milk tomorrow” is an action with a time. “Buy a new wardrobe” is an intent with no time. Today they share one list, so the second one sits there looking overdue-ish forever and makes the list feel dishonest.

What it costs. One column, one predicate beside isOverdue in lib/task-predicates.ts, one filter chip on /tasks, and one exclusion in the Today read. It reuses the domain, the project tag, the notes field and the whole task form.

04 The neglect sweep, and where its output lands

This is the one repair the last report ranked first, and it stays first. Dispatch already promises a cron that watches for neglect. There is no such cron. The threshold editor on a domain writes a number that nothing reads, into a table with no rows and no writers.

lib/services/today.ts:270cadenceThresholdDays — the parser exists and works. Its only caller feeds the number back into the edit field it came from.
lib/services/today.ts:177buildCadenceLines — computed into the Today digest every request. No component renders it. Dead output, live cost.
app/(authed)/domains/domain-row.tsx:75Help text describing “the observations cron” to the user. That cron does not exist.
app/api/cron/Holds caldav, reminders, sweep. sweep only reconciles stuck captures.

05 What the pages are missing

Every list page today opens with a title and a count of rows. A count of rows is the one fact you can already get by looking at the rows. The pages that feel thin are thin because the header states the obvious and withholds the interesting.

The design collision, stated honestly

ADR-0042 settled what the page header may carry: a 36px title, and a measure — a figure and the word it counts, at a flat 14px. ADR-0046 added facts — identity attributes, also flat. Both decisions were deliberately quiet, so that “a list page can never out-shout the day”. A row of big numbers is a third reading type, and it does not fit either slot.

06 Two answers the task form cannot take

Two gaps in one form. It cannot say which project a task belongs to, and it cannot say “every Tuesday and Saturday”. They land in the same three-column grid, and the fix for one makes room for the other.

A project you cannot put anything in

If a project is a tag for tasks, applying that tag is the only thing a project has to do. The column exists, the service accepts it, the filter reads it — and no screen can set it.

app/(authed)/tasks/task-fields.tsxThe task form has seven fields: title, notes, due date, due time, domain, repeats, priority. No project.
lib/schemas/task.tsCreateTaskFormSchema has no project_id, so even a hand-posted form field would be stripped before the service saw it.
lib/services/tasks.tscreateTask already accepts project_id. The write path is finished. Only the form is missing.
lib/services/capture/resolve.tsThe one and only writer: capture matches a project name inside the captured text. A guess is the sole way a task ever gets a project.
app/(authed)/tasks/task-filters.tsxA “project” filter on /tasks — a filter over a value you are not allowed to set.
app/(authed)/projects/[id]/page.tsxThe project page loads milestones and domains. It never loads tasks. A project has no idea what work belongs to it.
So the state today is: capture can guess a project onto a task, you cannot correct that guess, you cannot set one by hand, and no project surface shows you the result. The filter is the only evidence the relationship exists at all.

Where the field goes — and why Repeats has to move

The meta area is a three-column grid in two groups: Due date · Due time · the relative chips, then Domain · Repeats · Priority. All six slots are taken. The chips are one of those slots, not spare room — so there is nowhere to put a seventh control.

The way out is not to squeeze. It is that Repeats is about to need a whole row of its own — see below — which frees exactly the slot Project wants. The two requests solve each other.

Adding a task from a project

The same dialog, opened from the project page, with the Project field pre-filled and locked. Nothing new is designed and nothing is duplicated — ADR-0040 says there is one task form, and this keeps that true. A locked select still has to post its value, so the field renders disabled beside a hidden input carrying the id.

Why the project page has to list tasks too. An “Add task” button on a page that then shows you nothing is a trapdoor. The button and the list ship together, or neither ships.
lib/schemas/task.tsAdd project_id to CreateTaskFormSchema, with the .or(z.literal("")) treatment every optional FormData field needs.
app/(authed)/tasks/task-fields.tsxA Project select takes the slot Repeats vacates. New projects prop, new lockProject prop.
app/(authed)/tasks/task-dialog.tsx · actions.tsThread the project list in and the chosen id out.
app/(authed)/projects/[id]/page.tsxLoad the project's tasks. Today it loads milestones and domains only.

app/(authed)/tasks/task-list.tsx:62The optimistic builder hardcodes project_id: null. It has to carry the real value once the form can set one.

Repeats needs a Custom rule

“Weekly on Tuesdays and Saturdays” cannot be said today. RECURRENCE_PATTERNS is a flat list of seven literals — daily, weekdays, weekly, every 2 weeks, monthly, every 6 months, yearly — and the select offers exactly those.

The column is already ready. tasks.recurrence_rule is plain text, and lib/recurrence.ts says so in its own opening comment: the vocabulary was kept small for v1, and richer rules can be added “without a migration.” This is the one feature on the whole plan that needs no SQL.

The rule format

A narrow extension, not a standard: weekly:tu,sa — the word weekly, a colon, then two-letter weekday codes. It reads correctly in a database client, it parses in about ten lines, and it leaves all seven existing literals valid exactly as they are.

Rejected: full RFC 5545 (FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=TU,SA). It is the right answer for an app that syncs recurrence with a calendar. Dispatch does not, and adopting the grammar invites every other part of it — counts, intervals, month-day sets — one support request at a time.

The real cost is not the date maths. Advancing to the next listed weekday is a loop that runs at most seven times. The cost is that three places currently assume a rule is one of the seven literals, and all three fail quietly — no error, just wrong behaviour.
lib/task-interaction/apply-intent.ts:60Guards the optimistic roll with isRecurrencePattern. An unknown rule skips the guard, so ticking the checkbox would show the task completing instead of rolling forward — then snap back when the server disagrees.
app/(authed)/tasks/task-row.tsx:244recurrenceLabel returns null for anything unknown, so a custom task would silently lose its ↻ chip and look like a one-off.
lib/schemas/task.ts · lib/schemas/capture.tsThree z.enum(RECURRENCE_PATTERNS) sites would reject the string outright. These fail loudly, which is the easy kind.
lib/recurrence.tsnextDueDate gains a parse step and one branch. periodStart treats a custom weekly as a Monday-anchored week — same as plain weekly.
lib/recurrence.tsAside: periodStart and isCurrentlyDoneRecurring have no live caller. Their only consumer was the checklist schema §08 retires.

07 Routines: edit, and fix yesterday

Two gaps, one small and one that breaks a stated rule.

Editing a routine

There is no edit. The page offers create, archive and delete only, and deleting warns that the whole completion history goes with it — so a typo in a routine name currently costs you the streak. The server side is already there: UpdateRoutineSchema exists and is complete. What is missing is an updateRoutineAction and an edit mode on the row, exactly like the one /domains already has.

Ticking a past day

The service can already do it — setCompletion(sb, id, date, done) takes a date, and ToggleCompletionSchema accepts one. The server action deliberately refuses to: it derives the date from the server clock and its comment says the date is “never trusted from the client.” Backfill means letting the client name a date, which is a real reversal of that rule and needs to be written down, not slipped in.

How to keep the rule mostly intact. Accept a date from the client, then bound it server-side: it must be a real calendar date, not in the future, and not older than the 30 days the grid already shows. The client picks which of the visible squares to tick; it never gets to invent a date the UI is not already displaying. Streaks and rates recompute from the completion log, so no stored total goes stale.
app/(authed)/routines/actions.tsAdd updateRoutineAction; widen toggleCompletionAction to take a bounded date.
app/(authed)/routines/routine-row.tsxEdit mode on the row; the 30 grid squares become buttons instead of decoration.
lib/routine-stats.tsAlready computes streak, longest streak, 7-day count and the grid. Nothing to add for the band except the two percentages.

08 Retire now, drop later

Two of the answers pull against each other: milestones and the billing fields are agreed removals, but nothing is to be dropped from the database yet. Both are satisfiable — they are different phases, not the same one.

Phase A — retire in code (this plan)

  • Stop reading and writing the column or table.
  • Remove its UI, its form field, its service function.
  • Remove it from the row schema, so it leaves the select.
  • The rows stay in Postgres, untouched and reachable.

Reversible in an afternoon. Nothing is lost.

Phase B — drop in SQL (a later, separate patch)

  • One migration dropping whatever Phase A proved unnecessary.
  • Only after living without it long enough to be sure.
  • Its own ADR, following the ADR-0007 precedent.

Done — 6 Sep 2026, migration 20260906160000_phase_b_drops.sql, ADR-0056. Not reversible: there is no down script.

10 Every question, and how it was answered

Six questions were put to the owner on 21 Aug 2026; two more opened with the task-form work. All eight are settled and every recommendation was accepted. They are kept here with their reasoning, because the reasoning is the part worth re-reading. All eight were built as settled, with one change: O3’s note source became notes.updated_at, not created_at.

O1Is “don’t drop anything yet” about the database only?

Milestones and the billing fields were agreed removals, and then nothing was to be dropped. §07 reads that as: pull them out of the code now, leave the rows in Postgres, drop them in a later patch.

Settled: yes — Phase A now, Phase B never scheduled until you have lived without it.

O2Which pages actually get a stat band?

Four are clear wins and three are guesses (§05). Adding a band to a page whose numbers you would never read is exactly the mistake the jerad-ops 2.0 release was written about.

Settled: ship /routines, /domains and /tasks. Leave /projects, /journal and /notes until those three have been used for a while.

O3Does writing a note count as touching its domain?

Once notes carry a domain, they become a fifth candidate source for the neglect sweep’s last-touch date. Counting them makes a domain look tended when all you did was think about it. Not counting them means a week of notes about Health still reads as neglect.

Settled: count it. Attention is what this measure is for, and thinking about something is attention.

built as notes.updated_at, not created_at (72541e1). Create-time only would have meant that editing an old note, or filing one into a domain, counted as nothing. Journal is still not a source: journal_entries has no domain link.

O4How far back may a routine day be ticked?

The row already draws 30 days. A wider window needs a date picker and a reason.

Settled: 30 days — exactly the squares already on screen, enforced server-side.

O5How many tasks does a project row show?

Open tasks only, or everything including done? Always visible, or behind a disclosure?

Settled: open tasks only, up to five, then “+N more”. Done work belongs on the project page, not in the list.

O6Which ADRs does this plan owe?

The next free number is 0053 — 0052 is note attachments. Three decisions here are hard to reverse, surprising without context, and the result of a real trade-off, which is the bar the repo uses.

Settled: one for the stat band as a third reading type against ADR-0042/0046; one for a client-named completion date on routines; one recording the jerad-ops 2.0 read — what was taken, what was refused, and that Linear owns delivery history. AGENTS.md requires the third one anyway.

O7Where do the relative chips go?

An earlier draft of this plan moved Repeats into the third slot of the Due row. That was wrong: the third slot is the today · +1d · +1w · reset cluster, not free space. All six slots are occupied.

Settled: the chips do not move at all. Repeats leaves the filing row and takes a row of its own, because the Custom weekday strip cannot fit in a third of a row. Project fills the slot Repeats vacates. Three groups, one question each: when · how often · where and how much.

O8How far does the Custom grammar go?

weekly:tu,sa covers “weekly on these weekdays”. It does not cover “every 2 weeks on Tuesday and Saturday”, or “the last Friday of the month”. Each of those is a small extension on its own, and together they are RFC 5545.

Settled: stop at weekly-on-weekdays. Add an interval only when a real task needs it, not in advance. Keep weekdays as its own literal rather than rewriting it to weekly:mo,tu,we,th,fr — the rows already stored say weekdays, and a rewrite buys nothing.

Status. Built. All nine phases of §09 shipped on 6 Sep 2026, one commit each, on main; four migrations (notes.domain_id, tasks.someday, the widened recurrence check, notes.updated_at) are applied to the live database, and the three ADRs the plan owed — 0053, 0054, 0055 — are written.

What the plan got wrong, and what changed after it. P7 was not free of SQL: a CHECK constraint pinned tasks.recurrence_rule to seven literals. buildCadenceLines was not dead code when P1 ran; it was removed separately once its two readers were moved onto the counts directly, which also dropped the cadence array from the widget JSON. And O3’s note source became notes.updated_at rather than created_at, so filing or editing a note now revives a quiet domain — the migration the plan deferred.

Phase B ran too. On the same day, against the plan’s own “only after living without it long enough to be sure”: every candidate except milestones was already empty, and engagement_type held its own default on all nine rows, so there was nothing left to be sure about. milestones lost two rows — one done, one open — dropped on an explicit instruction rather than by accident. ADR-0056 records it. The drop is not reversible.

What is deliberately still open. Journal is still not a last-touch source: journal_entries has no domain link, and inventing one is a guess. §05’s remaining stat-band candidates were left alone on purpose.
