Jerad shipped Dashboard 2.0 as a subtraction release: the governing question was which features had earned their place after months of daily use. Read that way, the useful output isn't a port list — it's the same question turned on Dispatch. The headline is not a feature. Dispatch's largest gap is a promise it already made and never wired: PRODUCT.md principle #3 is “notice absence, not just presence,” and the positioning claims “a cron job actively watches for [neglect].” There is no such cron. The threshold editor writes a number nothing reads, into a table with zero rows and zero writers.

01 The article's own ledger

Three months in, Jerad asked whether the dashboard beat Todoist or whether he was just attached to it. That question produced the release.

Killed

The nightly shutdown & scoreboard

Five checks at 20:30, a win rate, a weekly recap. Used for two weeks. Being asked to reopen and grade the day after already switching off for the evening turned it into a chore with nothing to like about it.

Replaced by one optional line: tomorrow's focus.

“A feature only removes friction if it connects to a challenge you're actually facing.” — Jerad Hill

Kept & sharpened

Three states, computed not curated

Everything lives in one of three states — my move, waiting on someone, gone quiet. Each client carries its own check-in cadence, so silence surfaces itself.

Nothing on the Work page is draggable. No statuses to maintain. Progress, recency, overdue counts and who holds the ball are all derived. Status first, tasks second.

Learned

Don't rebuild a foundation that's holding

A briefing bug (“17 days since a Field Notes article”) nearly triggered a full domain restructure. The actual fix: let a task update a domain, not just a content item or project. One prompt.

His conclusion: tolerating a structure that is merely imperfect costs almost nothing next to tearing out one that still works.

04 The broken circuit

This is the finding worth the read. Dispatch has the edit form, the storage, the “Mark shipped” button and the destination table — and no wire between them.

Today — an open circuit

R1 — the circuit closed

The evidence

app/api/cron/Holds caldav, reminders, sweep. sweep only reconciles stuck captures — it has nothing to do with neglect.
lib/services/today.ts:270cadenceThresholdDays() is read at exactly one non-test call site: the domains page, to refill the edit field it came from.
app/(authed)/domains/domain-row.tsx:75Field help text reads “before the observations cron flags it.” That cron does not exist.
lib/services/domains.tsmarkDomainShipped() stamps last_shipped_at. Nothing anywhere reads the column.
observations · activity_logBoth tables exist in lib/database.types.ts with zero readers and zero writers across lib/, app/, components/.
Why this one first. It is the only item where Jerad's v2 fix and Dispatch's own unmet promise are the same change. It adds no page, no nav and no vocabulary — one service, one cron route, one extra CadenceLine. And “a task tends its domain” is precisely the fix the article spent a page earning.

05 Two states, where the reference now has three

Dispatch can tell you what is due. It cannot tell you what is yours — an open task blocked on someone else looks identical to one waiting on you, and ages into overdue exactly the same way.

07 Ranked

Positioned by what each costs against what it changes about opening the app in the morning.

R1

Wire the neglect sweep Dispatch already claims

do first

Domain last-touch = MAX of four sources, one of them being a completed task in that domain — the article's exact fix. One number, compared against the threshold the editor already writes. No new page, no new nav, no new vocabulary; one more CadenceLine on Today.

R2

The third task state

one decision for you

Add waiting_on text + waiting_since date, both nullable, and derive waiting as waiting_since !== null && status === "open". Recommended over a third enum value: every predicate in lib/task-predicates.ts tests status !== "done", so adding "waiting" to the enum would silently change isOverdue and isDueToday for blocked tasks. The reference does use a third status — this is a deliberate deviation, and the kind ADR-0007 exists to record.

  • newsupabase/migrations/… — two nullable columns on tasks
  • modlib/task-predicates.ts — isWaiting beside its siblings
  • modapp/(authed)/tasks/ — a Waiting filter (ADR-0029) and the one-tap flip
  • modlib/services/observations.ts — second rule: waiting ≥ 7 days
open call nullable pair vs third enum valuemigration 1
R3

Computed project state, worst-first

Port the derivation, not the page. urgency.ts is ~60 pure lines and its best idea is parentUrgency: a domain can never read calmer than a project inside it. Derive once server-side so two surfaces cannot disagree. Then /projects orders worst-first instead of alphabetically, and each row carries recency and counts. Status first, tasks second.

  • modlib/services/projects.ts — rollup in listProjects, beside the existing milestoneProgress

R5

Delete what stays dead

last

Once R1–R4 have claimed observations and activity_log, drop the four that remain unclaimed, plus the orphan schemas. Deliberately last: deleting first would remove the tables R1 is about to need.

  • delsupabase/migrations/… — action_log, project_checklist_items, inventory_items, inventory_categories

The ADR either way. AGENTS.md requires a new ADR whenever a decision deviates from the reference. Whichever of R1–R5 gets green-lit, docs/adr/0052 should record this read: what was adopted, what was refused, and why daily_focus is not coming.

08 Explicitly not doing

The honesty pass cuts both ways. Each of these is a v2 feature Dispatch should decline, with the reason it declines.

✕Tomorrow's FocusDispatch's top3_for_date is the same idea, already scoped to a calendar date, and holds three rather than one. Porting daily_focus would be a downgrade wearing a release note.
✕The Daily Rule, shutdown, score, recap, hedgeNever built here. Jerad ran it two weeks and pulled it. This is confirmation of a cut, not a gap — worth recording precisely so it does not get proposed again.
✕Content pipeline, days_since_publishADR-0007 cut it because the reference was built for a content creator and this owner is a developer. Nothing in 2.0 changes that.
✕Companies / CRM hierarchyDispatch has People and no client companies. Take the silence mechanic (R4); leave the company layer.
✕Detail Pages v2, white canvas, icon rail, 800px gateDispatch owns its own design system across ADR-0013 and ADR-0039–0051. A different app's redesign is not evidence about this one.
✕The Android quote widget appDispatch already ships /api/widget and installs as an iOS PWA. Different platform problem.
✕Photo-forward libraryCapture is text-only by iron rule #4 and ADR-0017. A photo library would mean an attachment pipeline — a foundation change, which is the exact move the article talked itself out of.
Worth stealing without writing any code: Jerad's BACKLOG.md keeps deferred work with a felt-pain gate — something leaves the list when its absence actually hurts, not because it is listed. And his guardrail against version bloat: before building, have the model restate the problem back to you. More than once he read it and realised it was a momentary friction point, not a problem.
